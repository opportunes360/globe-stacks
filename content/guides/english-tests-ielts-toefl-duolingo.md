Many scholarships want proof that your English is strong enough for study in English. You show it with a test score. Three tests are accepted most often: **IELTS**, **TOEFL** and the **Duolingo English Test**.

We read the official test pages and the British Council Tanzania pages on 19 November 2026. Where a page did not say something, we write **not confirmed**. Prices change, so check the official page before you pay.

## First: do you need a test at all?

Maybe not. Some scholarships and universities skip the test if your recent schooling was taught in English. For example:

- **Amherst** says you can skip the test if your most recent two years of school were taught in English. Many Tanzanian secondary schools teach in English, but Amherst has not confirmed that your school counts. Email them before you pay.
- **Berea** asks everyone for a score, with no exceptions, even if you already speak English.
- **MEXT Japan** does not need an English or Japanese certificate.

So read the English rule of each scholarship first.

## IELTS

**What it is.** IELTS has four parts: Listening (about 30 minutes), Reading (60 minutes), Writing (60 minutes) and Speaking (11 to 14 minutes, face to face with an examiner). There are two versions, **Academic** and **General Training**. For university study, choose **Academic**. Oxford, for example, recommends IELTS Academic and says it does not accept IELTS General, IELTS Life Skills or IELTS Online.

**Scores.** You get a band from 0 to 9 for each part and an overall band, in whole or half bands (for example 6.5). The overall band is the average of the four.

**Booking in Tanzania** (British Council page):

- The only test centre is **Dar es Salaam**, and the test is **on a computer**.
- The fee for **IELTS Academic is TZS 902,000**. IELTS for UKVI (a version for some UK visa needs) is TZS 968,000.
- The fee includes the free online course **IELTS Ready Premium** and **five extra Test Report Forms** sent free to the universities you name.
- You upload a copy of your passport or national ID. If you are **under 18**, you need written consent from a parent or guardian.
- Results: the page says you can preview your results **1 to 5 days** after a computer test. (Another line on the same site mentions 13 days, so ask when you book.) Institutions need the official Test Report Form, not a photocopy.
- There is no limit on retakes.

Book here: <https://www.britishcouncil.or.tz/exam/ielts/book-test>

## TOEFL iBT

**What it is.** TOEFL is made by a company called ETS. In January 2026 the test changed. ETS lists four sections: Reading (about 30 minutes), Listening (about 29), Writing (about 23) and Speaking (about 8). The test adapts to you, so questions change with your answers. There are **no scheduled breaks**. You can take it at a test centre or at home with a live proctor (the Home Edition).

**Scores.** Since **21 January 2026** the scores are on a **1 to 6 scale** in half points. The overall score is the average of your four sections, rounded to the nearest half. For two years you will also see a comparable score on the old 0 to 120 scale. ETS gives this guide for tests taken before 2026:

| New scale | Old total (0 to 120) |
| --- | --- |
| 6 | 114 or more |
| 5 | 95 or more |
| 4.5 | 86 or more |
| 4 | 72 or more |

If a scholarship still lists a 0 to 120 score, do not convert anything. Your report shows both.

**Cost and booking.** ETS has a fee page, but the table of fees by country did not load for us, so the **fee for Tanzania is not confirmed**. Your fee includes up to four free official score reports. Extra reports cost money (ETS's pages show US$25 on one page and US$29 on another). ETS's discount voucher is only for high school seniors in the United States.

We could not confirm a TOEFL test centre in Tanzania, or whether the Home Edition is open to you. Check when you start to book: <https://www.ets.org/oets-toefl/register>

**Careful with old advice.** The test changed in January 2026, so check that any guide or video you use describes the new test. The ETS pages are the safest source.

## Duolingo English Test

**What it is.** You take it online, at home, in about one hour, and you do not travel to a centre. About 5 minutes is set-up and ID check, about 45 minutes is the adaptive test (the questions change with your answers), and about 10 minutes is a short writing sample and speaking sample. Universities can read and watch these samples.

**Scores.** The overall score runs from **10 to 160** in steps of 5, with four subscores (Literacy, Comprehension, Conversation and Production). Results come in **2 days**, or 12 hours if you buy faster results. You can send your result to an unlimited number of universities at no extra cost.

**What you need at home** (from Duolingo's 2026 guide): a computer with camera, microphone and speakers; a charged phone to check your ID and record your screen; your **real government photo ID** (copies and photos are not allowed); internet of at least 2 Mbps down and 1 Mbps up; and a quiet, private room for one hour with no headphones, notes or VPN. You must start within 21 days of buying the test.

**Cost.** The official guide we read does not print a price, and other websites disagree (we saw US$49 to US$70). So the fee is **not confirmed**. Check the price on the Buy page at <https://englishtest.duolingo.com> before you pay.

**Important:** not every scholarship accepts Duolingo. Check first.

## How to choose

1. **Start with the scholarship.** List the tests it accepts and the minimum score.
2. **Check the deadline.** Add time for the test date, your results and sending the score. IELTS in Tanzania means a trip to Dar es Salaam.
3. **Check the price in your own money.** Do not pay for a second test until you know the first score is not enough.
4. **Pick the test you can do best.** The free practice tests below will show you.
5. **Book early.** Test places can fill up.

## How to prepare for free

**IELTS.** The British Council Tanzania prepare page lists free practice tests (you can download the tests, blank answer sheets and answers), free apps, free video tutorials and a free computer-delivered familiarisation test. The course IELTS Ready Premium comes free once you book. Start here: <https://www.britishcouncil.or.tz/exam/ielts/prepare>

**TOEFL.** ETS offers free practice sets and a free practice test. Its free full-length Practice Test 1 (a PDF) matches tests from 21 January 2026. Older free material lacks the newest Writing task, so use this first: <https://www.ets.org/pdfs/toefl/toefl-ibt-full-length-practice-test-1.pdf>

**Duolingo.** Make a free account at englishtest.duolingo.com and take the free practice test as often as you like at <https://englishtest.duolingo.com/quick>. It is shorter than the real test and not proctored, and its score is only an estimate. Duolingo also says you do not need special test preparation, because the test is meant to show your real English.

**Everyday English.** All three tests reward real English, not tricks. Duolingo's official guide suggests speaking to yourself in English, writing your daily tasks in English, reading news in English, and watching films and listening to podcasts while repeating what you hear. Try 20 to 30 minutes a day for a few weeks.

## Do not do this

- **Do not buy a test before you read the scholarship rule.** Each one names the tests it accepts.
- **Do not use an old score.** Scores are valid for two years, and Berea almost never accepts older ones.
- **Do not pay anyone who promises a score.** A real score comes only from the official test. See our guide on [how scholarships work and how to spot scams](/guides/how-scholarships-work-and-how-to-spot-scams/).

A student visa is permission to enter a country to study. You apply for it **after** you are admitted or awarded a scholarship, not before. Every country has its own rules, so this guide explains the general pattern.

## The usual order

1. **Get your admission letter or scholarship award.** You cannot apply for a student visa without it.
2. **Find the official visa instructions.** Use the embassy or government immigration website of the country you are going to. Do not rely on social media posts or someone's message.
3. **Make your document list** from that official page.
4. **Submit the application** the way the embassy says: online, by post, or at a visa centre.
5. **Attend any appointment or interview.**
6. **Wait for the decision**, then check the visa for mistakes.

## Documents usually asked for

Your list will depend on the country, but these are common:

- A passport that is valid long enough. Check how long it must remain valid.
- The admission letter or scholarship letter.
- Passport photos in the exact size the embassy requires.
- Proof that you can pay for your studies and living costs. For a full scholarship, the scholarship letter is often the proof.
- Your certificates and transcripts.
- A medical check or vaccination records, for some countries.
- A police clearance certificate, for some countries.
- The visa fee receipt.
- Fingerprints taken at an appointment, for some countries.

## If you have a government scholarship

Many government scholarships help with the visa step. They may send an invitation letter or a document you need, and tell you which embassy or visa centre to use. Read your award letter carefully and **follow its instructions exactly.**

## Common mistakes

- **Applying too late.** Visas can take weeks. Apply as soon as the official instructions allow.
- **Missing or wrong documents.** Use the embassy's checklist, tick off every item, and keep copies.
- **Names that do not match** across passport, certificates and letters.
- **Using unofficial sources.** Fake visa websites and "agents" are common. Always start from the embassy's own website.
- **Paying people who promise a guaranteed visa.** Nobody can guarantee a visa except the embassy that decides.
- **Not telling the truth.** Never lie or submit false documents. It can lead to a ban.

## At an interview

- Be calm and answer honestly.
- Know your course, your university and your plans.
- Bring original documents and copies.

## Keep everything

Save digital copies of your application, receipts and emails. If something goes wrong, you will have proof.

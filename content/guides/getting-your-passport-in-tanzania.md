Most scholarships ask for a passport, and some ask for it **when you apply**. The good news is that you can get one yourself, step by step, without paying an agent. This guide gives you the whole process in one place.

Everything here comes from the Tanzania Immigration Department's own passport guide (signed by the Commissioner General of Immigration on 17 April 2018) and from the Ministry of Home Affairs customer service charter (June 2023). When these sources do not say something, we write "not confirmed" instead of guessing.

## Which passport do you need?

You need the **Ordinary Passport** (*Pasipoti ya Kawaida*). The Immigration Department says any citizen of Tanzania can have one for travel abroad.

- **Validity:** 10 years.
- **Cover:** sky blue.
- **Where it works:** you can travel to any country with it. A visa may still be needed (see our [student visa guide](/guides/student-visa-basics/)).

Service and Diplomatic passports are only for government leaders listed in the law, so you will not use them.

## Why you should apply now

Our scholarship pages show how often a passport comes up:

- **Chinese Government Scholarship:** a valid passport, and the information page must be valid at least 12 months after your study start date.
- **CyprusAid and Türkiye Burslari:** a valid passport or identity document.
- **Morocco (AMCI):** a certified copy of the passport data page and two recent colour photos.
- **Berea College:** the passport photo page, sent before it issues the form you need for your US student visa.

The government's promised time to issue a passport is **5 days** at the Dar es Salaam headquarters or the Zanzibar main office, and **10 days** at regional immigration offices. The charter does not say when that clock starts, and it does not say how long collection takes. So plan for more time than that, and do not wait until you have won.

## Documents you need before you start

Get these ready first. Names on every document must match the birth certificate.

**Everyone needs:**

1. Your **birth certificate** (or a *Tajnisi* certificate).
2. The **birth certificate or affidavit of one parent** (or a *Tajnisi* certificate).
3. Your **national ID (NIDA)**.
4. A copy of a **parent's passport**, if a parent has one.
5. An **application letter** (*barua ya maombi*).

**Also bring, as it applies to you:**

- **Proof of what you do.** If you have no job, you must prove why you want to travel. As a student, bring your **educational certificates**, because the guide lists them for study travel. A scholarship offer or admission letter is not mentioned in the guide, so it is not confirmed whether it helps.
- **A letter from your local government office** (*Serikali ya Mtaa*) that confirms where you live.
- **A deed poll or an ordinary affidavit** if your names are written differently on different documents. Which one you need depends on how they differ.

The Chief Passport Control Officer can ask for more documents if there are doubts about your application.

**If you are under 18:** you must come to the office with a **legal parent or guardian**. Not confirmed: whether an under-18 applicant needs a NIDA number.

**Not confirmed:** the size and background rules for the photo you upload.

### Two parts of the form that people miss

- **Section 7** must be filled in and signed by your **guarantors** (*wadhamini*).
- **Section 9** must be filled in and **stamped** by a Magistrate, Judge, Advocate or Commissioner for Oaths.
- If you use a **birth affidavit** instead of a birth certificate, it needs the required **stamp duty**.

## How to apply, step by step

You start online and finish at an immigration office.

1. Open the Immigration Department's online passport application page: **eservices.immigration.go.tz/online/passport**. You can also reach it from www.immigration.go.tz under e-Services, then "Online Passport Application".
2. Choose **OMBI JIPYA** (new application). The other two options, **ENDELEZA OMBI** and **ANGALIA OMBI**, let you continue an unfinished form or check progress.
3. Choose your **application type**. If you have never had a passport, pick the first-time option. **Be careful here.** If you choose the wrong type, your first payment cannot be moved. You must fill in a new form and pay again.
4. Fill in your details **in capital letters**, exactly as on your birth certificate.
5. Attach your photo and the documents the form asks for.
6. Check everything on the review page and tick the **Declaration** box.
7. You receive a **control number** for the first payment of **TZS 20,000**. Pay it and print the form.
8. Take the printed form and your documents to the **nearest regional Immigration office, the Kurasini office, or the Zanzibar Head Office**.
9. An officer checks your documents. If everything is correct, you receive a **second control number** for **TZS 130,000**. Pay it.
10. After the second payment your application is accepted. Next you give your **photo and fingerprints** (biometrics).
11. You are told the date to **collect your passport** at the Immigration office.

If you make a mistake on the printed form, fix it in **ink** and sign next to the new entry.

## What it costs

| What | Fee in Tanzania |
| --- | --- |
| New Ordinary Passport (first payment TZS 20,000 + second payment TZS 130,000) | **TZS 150,000** |
| Emergency Travel Document | TZS 20,000 |
| Damaged passport | TZS 300,000 |
| Lost passport, first time | TZS 500,000 |
| Lost passport, second time | TZS 750,000 |

A renewal (when your passport is full or expired) uses the same process and the standard fees. The guide gives no separate renewal fee.

These are the figures in the Immigration Department's guide, which is dated 2018. The department's live fee page would not load for us, so we cannot confirm that the amounts have not changed. The control number you receive always shows the exact amount you must pay. If it is different from the table, pay the control number, because that is what the system uses.

### How to pay safely

- **Pay only with the control number** for your application.
- In Tanzania you can pay by **mobile money or bank**.
- **Never give fee money to an Immigration officer** to pay for you.
- Your passport stays the property of the government. The government can recall it at any time.

If your printed passport has a mistake and you must apply again, the guide says you fill in a new form and pay the full **TZS 150,000**. That is why you should check every detail before you submit.

## If something goes wrong

**Lost passport.** Do these in order:

1. Report it to the **police** and get a Police Loss Report.
2. Publish a notice in **two newspapers**, one in English and one in Swahili.
3. Make a **legal declaration** (*tamko la kisheria*) about the loss.
4. Make a new online application and follow every step again.
5. Tell Immigration: phone **+255 262-323542** or **+255 262-323532**, or email **lostpassport@immigration.go.tz**. Give your full name, passport number (if you remember it), the date and place of loss, and what happened.

A Passport Control Officer will interview you before a replacement is issued.

**Damaged passport.** Fill in the online form, write a letter that explains how it was damaged, and bring the damaged passport.

**Changed name.** Use a deed poll or an ordinary affidavit, depending on how the names differ.

**Urgent travel.** The **Emergency Travel Document** (*Shahada ya Dharura ya Kusafiria*) is for people who cannot get their passport in time. It costs TZS 20,000, is valid for **one trip only** and is issued at regional and district Immigration offices. The guide says it is issued within 24 hours after payment, and the charter says one working day. Do not use it as a plan for studying abroad. Start your passport early instead.

## Avoid middlemen

You do not need an agent. Every step above is something you can do yourself, and the only payments are the two control numbers.

- The Immigration guide warns that fees must never go to an officer. It does not mention agents, so this is our advice: do not pay anyone you cannot check, and do not give your documents or ID numbers to strangers online.
- Anyone who says they can "speed up" the passport for extra money is not using the official payment system.
- Read our guide on [how to spot scholarship scams](/guides/how-scholarships-work-and-how-to-spot-scams/).

## When you get it

- Check that your **name, date of birth and spelling** match your certificates exactly. A small difference can cause big problems with visas and scholarships.
- Make a **colour scan** of the photo page and keep it with your documents.
- Write the **expiry date** in your phone and set a reminder a year before.

## Your checklist

- [ ] I have my birth certificate and a parent's birth certificate or affidavit.
- [ ] I have my NIDA ID.
- [ ] I have my application letter and certificates.
- [ ] I have a letter from my local government office.
- [ ] My guarantors have signed Section 7, and an authorised officer has stamped Section 9.
- [ ] I chose the correct application type.
- [ ] I paid only with control numbers.
- [ ] I checked every detail before I submitted.
- [ ] I scanned my passport photo page.

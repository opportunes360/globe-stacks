Some universities want a test score before they read your application. In the USA the two big tests are the **SAT** and the **ACT**. This guide uses only what we read on the official College Board and ACT websites. Where we could not confirm something, we say "not confirmed".

**Dates and fees were checked on 19 November 2026.** They change, so confirm on the official pages (listed at the bottom) before you pay.

## The short answer

- **Not every scholarship needs a test.** Only some US colleges on this site ask for the SAT or ACT.
- **If a college requires a score, it usually accepts either test.** Choose one. You do not need both.
- **Book early.** Deadlines come weeks before test day.
- **Never pay anyone to "guarantee" a score or a seat.** Register yourself on the official website.

## What are the SAT and the ACT?

Both test reading, writing and maths. US colleges use them to compare students from different schools and countries.

**The SAT** (College Board): two sections, Reading and Writing (64 minutes) and Math (70 minutes), so 2 hours 14 minutes in total. Scores run from 400 to 1600. You take it on a laptop or tablet with the Bluebook app.

**The ACT**: since September 2025 your composite score is based on English, math and reading. Science and writing are **optional** and cost extra. ACT made the test shorter from February 2026, but the exact timing and number of questions were not on the pages we read: **not confirmed**.

### What this means for scholarships open now

- **Berea** closes on 30 November (see its entry). Both December tests come after that, so Berea needs a score you already have or another accepted English test.
- **Yale and Dartmouth** (score required) and **Amherst** (optional) have regular deadlines in early January 2027. The tests still open for them are the SAT on 5 December (late registration until 24 November) and the ACT on 11-12 December (late registration until 27 November). The next SAT is 6 March 2027, too late for these.
- Our Yale entry says January or February sittings are generally in time for its regular round. The ACT on 12-13 February 2027 is in that window, but we did **not** confirm that Yale accepts that date. Ask Yale first.
- If you miss these dates, the same score helps next year's round, so start preparing now.

## What does it cost?

**SAT** (fees through June 2027): $68 plus a $43 international fee. Late registration $38. Change test centre $34. Cancel $34, or $44 after the change deadline. A few centres in other countries charge an extra $24; none in the list we read are in Tanzania, but College Board says to ask your centre. Your first 4 score reports are free if you order them within 9 days after the test. Extra reports cost $15 each.

**ACT:** $188.50 for English, math and reading. With science $198.50. With writing $213.50. With both $223.50. Late registration $42. Changing the date or centre $49. Reports to a 5th and 6th college $20 each. Local taxes may apply.

**Fee waivers and shilling prices: not confirmed.** The College Board waiver link we saw is in its section for school staff. Ask College Board or ACT directly, and never pay a stranger for a "discount".

## How to register from Tanzania

1. **Make an account** with College Board (SAT) or MyACT (ACT). Use your **full legal name exactly as on your ID**.
2. **Choose a date and test centre.** We could not confirm the Tanzanian centres on the official pages. Some school websites mention Dar es Salaam and Moshi, but centres and seats change, so check inside the registration page. ACT centres open only some dates.
3. **Upload a photo and pay.** For the ACT, the photo is due by the late deadline.
4. **Get your device ready** (SAT). You need a laptop or tablet with Bluebook, or ask to borrow one in time.
5. **Save your admission ticket** and bring your ID.

**ID rules (SAT).** Bring a valid, unexpired, original photo ID, not a photocopy or a phone screen. It can be government-issued or from the school you attend now. If you are 21 or older, only a government ID works. The page lists special rules for some countries but not Tanzania, so the general rules apply. A **passport** is the safest choice: see [getting your passport in Tanzania](/guides/getting-your-passport-in-tanzania/). Missing ID can mean no test and no refund.

**Sending scores.** The college must receive your score from the testing company. Choose colleges when you register and look up each college's code on its admissions page.

## Prepare for free

College Board says all of these are free:

- **Bluebook:** full-length practice tests in the real test app.
- **Khan Academy:** official SAT lessons, quizzes and a study plan.
- **Student Question Bank:** thousands of real questions, filtered by topic.
- **Question of the Day** and free live tutoring at Schoolhouse.world.

Free ACT practice: **not confirmed** (we saw only an "Official ACT Prep Guide" and did not check its price).

A simple plan: take a full practice test now, mark what you got wrong, study those topics for a few weeks, then test again before you register.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Waiting for the deadline, then paying late fees.
- A registration name that does not match your ID.
- Forgetting your ID or device.
- Booking a test too late for the college's deadline.
- Using a false or invalid ID. College Board says this can cancel your scores and get you banned.
- Paying agents who promise a "guaranteed score". Read [how to spot scams](/guides/how-scholarships-work-and-how-to-spot-scams/).

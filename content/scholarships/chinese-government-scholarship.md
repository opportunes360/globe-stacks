## What it is

The Chinese Government Scholarship is managed by the China Scholarship Council (CSC). You apply online on the official portal, **campuschina.org**, and your own government sends in or approves the application. For Tanzania this is the **Ministry of Education, Science and Technology** (Dodoma).

This page focuses on the **bachelor's** route, because Globe Stacks is for undergraduates. Master's and PhD applicants use the same system but have different age limits and extra documents.

## How Tanzania applies

Tanzania's Ministry of Education, Science and Technology publishes a yearly notice called "Scholarships Tenable in the People's Republic of China". The notice we could read in full (for 2024/25) says:

- The scholarships are fully funded and cover bachelor's, master's and doctoral study.
- You apply on campuschina.org, choose **Program Category "Type A"** and enter **Agency number 8341**. A wrong agency number means automatic disqualification.
- You then print the form and post it, with your supporting documents, to the Ministry before the deadline. The Ministry's address is: Permanent Secretary, Ministry of Education, Science and Technology, Government City, Mtumba, Afya Street, P.O. Box 10, 40479 Dodoma.

A third-party site reproducing the 2026/27 notice gave the same agency number (8341) and a deadline of **30 January 2026**. We could not open the official 2026/27 file, so treat that as a pointer, not as proof. **The agency number and the steps can change. Always use the number in the current Tanzanian notice.**

## What the official notices say about eligibility

The 2027/28 notices from Chinese embassies in other countries, and the Tanzanian notice for 2024/25, agree on the main rules:

- You must be a citizen of your country, in good physical and mental health, with a valid passport.
- **Bachelor's:** you must have completed high school (for Tanzania, A-level or equivalent) and be **under 25**. Check in the current notice which date your age is counted on.
- Master's: bachelor's degree and under 35. PhD: master's degree and under 40.
- Good academic results. South Africa's government sets a 60% minimum average for its own applicants. We found no Tanzanian minimum, so ask the Ministry.
- You cannot hold another Chinese government or university scholarship at the same time. Hiding this can lead to a three-year ban.

## The CSCA test (bachelor's applicants)

Bachelor's applicants must now submit a valid **CSCA** (China Scholastic Competency Assessment) score report. The notices say you must register and sit the test **before the application deadline**.

- Register at <https://www.csca.cn/>.
- Which subjects you take depends on the programme, so ask your chosen university.
- For September 2027 entry, South Africa's government lists test dates of **19 December 2026** and **23 January 2027**. Other notices say to register in November, December 2026 or January 2027. Check csca.cn for the dates and test centres available to you in Tanzania.
- A university page lists a fee of CNY 450 for one subject and CNY 700 for two or more. Confirm the fee on csca.cn.

Because the Ministry's deadline is usually in late January, **do not wait for Tanzania's notice before you plan your CSCA test.**

## Language

- Most bachelor's programmes are taught in **Chinese**. South Africa's notice says these need **HSK Level 3** or above.
- Programmes taught in English ask for proof such as IELTS or TOEFL, as each university decides.
- Applicants who study Chinese as their subject are exempt from the HSK rule.

## What it covers

The notices call it a full scholarship. The amounts below come from South Africa's higher education department, which reports what the Chinese government pays. **We did not find these amounts in a Tanzanian notice, so confirm them before you rely on them.**

- Tuition fees
- Student accommodation, or a housing subsidy
- Monthly living allowance: **CNY 2,500** for bachelor's students
- Medical insurance: CNY 800 per year

**International flights are not covered** by the Chinese side. Plan for your ticket and visa costs yourself.

## Documents you will usually need

- Passport information page (valid at least 12 months after your study start date)
- Certified copies of your CSEE and ACSEE certificates (or equivalent), with certified translations if they are not in English or Chinese
- Academic transcripts
- HSK or IELTS/TOEFL certificate, as the programme requires
- CSCA score report
- A **pre-admission document** from at least one Chinese university you applied to, such as an admission notice or invitation letter
- Study plan of at least **200 words** for bachelor's applicants
- Foreigner Physical Examination Form (for stays over six months; valid for six months)
- Non-criminal record certificate, usually issued within six months before you submit
- Birth certificate (listed in Tanzania's 2024/25 notice)
- Legal guardian's details in China, if you are under 18
- A portfolio, for art or design programmes

Tanzania's notice also asks for two recommendation letters from professors for postgraduate applicants. Check the current notice for any change.

## How to apply

1. Watch the Ministry's announcements page and the Chinese Embassy in Dar es Salaam for the 2027/28 notice. The Ministry's page is <https://www.moe.go.tz/> (see "Ufadhili").
2. Choose the universities and programmes you want on <https://www.campuschina.org/>. Check each programme's language and CSCA rules.
3. Book your CSCA test and, if needed, your HSK test.
4. Get a pre-admission document from your university.
5. Register on campuschina.org, choose **Type A**, enter the Tanzanian agency number from the current notice and submit.
6. Print the application and post it with your documents to the Ministry before its deadline.
7. Keep your phone and email active. Only nominated candidates are contacted, and some countries run an embassy interview afterwards.

If you change anything after submitting, withdraw the application, edit it and submit again. Otherwise the new version is not received.

## Other routes

Some Chinese universities offer their own scholarships, and CSC lists them on campuschina.org. Those have their own rules and deadlines, so read each university's page. This page covers the government route through the Ministry only.

> **Careful:** the Chinese government does not use agents for this scholarship, and the application is free. Some "agents" charge heavy fees for work you can do yourself. See [how to spot scams](/guides/how-scholarships-work-and-how-to-spot-scams/).

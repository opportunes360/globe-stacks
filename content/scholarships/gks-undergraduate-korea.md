> **Status on 18 November 2026:** the 2027 round (study starts in 2027) has closed. Students who applied are in the selection stages now. If you did not apply, plan for the next round. As of today the official site has not announced it.

## What it is

The Global Korea Scholarship for Undergraduates (GKS-U) is run by Korea's National Institute for International Education (NIIED). It pays for a bachelor's degree in Korea. You do not go straight into your degree. Everyone first spends **one year learning Korean**, then does a **four to six year** degree, so the scholarship lasts five to seven years. The language year and the degree are at the **same university**.

## Is Tanzania included?

Yes. Tanzania is on the official list of invited countries for the 2027 **embassy track**, with **3 places**: 1 in the General programme and 2 in R-GKS. The guidelines also say an embassy may choose not to recommend a candidate depending on local circumstances.

You apply through the Korean embassy route, not straight to NIIED. NIIED says it does not accept applications from individuals. The Embassy of the Republic of Korea in Tanzania is the contact:

- Phone: +255 22 211 6086 to 6088
- Email: embassy-tz@mofa.go.kr

We did not find a Tanzania-specific 2027 notice from the embassy, so we cannot tell you its own interview dates or document rules. The embassy's older GKS page has old numbers on it, so do not use it.

There is also a second route that does not depend on your country (see "Two ways to apply").

## Two ways to apply

**1. Embassy track.** You apply online through the Study in Korea system. There are two programmes that matter for Tanzanians:

- **General:** choose up to three universities, in order of preference. At least one must be a regional ("Type B") university.
- **R-GKS:** choose up to two Type B (regional) universities.

You choose only from the universities and departments in the official University Information file. Passing the selection does not guarantee a university place, because each university also decides in the last round. If every choice is a famous, very competitive university you can end up with no place, so include less crowded options.

**2. University track.** You apply to **one university and one department**, in the way that university says, not through the Study in Korea system. For bachelor's degrees this is the **UIC programme** (university-industry cooperation): science and engineering subjects such as software and AI, engineering, food science, biotechnology, statistics and environmental engineering, at ten universities. **UIC is open to citizens of all countries**, so you do not need to be on the invited list. There is also an associate degree route.

You cannot use both tracks at once. If you fail the first round of the embassy track you may then apply to a university track.

## Who can apply

- **Citizenship:** you must hold citizenship of an invited country (UIC is open to everyone except Korea). You and your parents must **not** hold Korean citizenship, and dual Korean citizenship is not allowed.
- **Age:** under 25, meaning born after 1 March 2002.
- **Education:** you finished high school or an associate degree, or you will by **31 December 2026**. Someone finishing in March 2027 could not apply. A bachelor's degree holder cannot apply.
- **Grades:** at least **80 out of 100**, or within the **top 20%** of your class, or a CGPA of 2.64/4.0 or higher (or the same level on a 4.3, 4.5 or 5.0 scale).
- **Health:** good mental and physical health for the whole programme, and nothing that would stop you travelling abroad.
- **Not allowed:** graduates of Korean high schools (including international schools), people who already got a Korean government degree scholarship, and former GKS scholars.

Tanzanian results are not usually given as a percentage or a GPA. The guidelines say that if your grades are not on an accepted scale, you must submit an official description of the grading system issued by your school or a government body such as the Ministry of Education, **and** a converted grade your school confirms. A conversion from a website is only valid if your school confirms it. Without it the committee cannot judge your grades.

## What it covers

From the official guidelines:

- **Tuition:** NIIED pays up to 5 million won. The university covers any amount above that, and the admission fee.
- **Korean language programme fee:** 5.2 million won a year.
- **Airfare:** an economy flight ticket (actual cost) to Korea.
- **Monthly allowance:** 13.56 million won a year during the language year and **14.4 million won a year** during the degree. It is meant to cover living costs, housing, health insurance, the TOPIK exam fee and a settlement allowance. You pay for your **accommodation** from this allowance.

Not covered: travel within your own country, and travel insurance for the trip to Korea. Amounts may change.

If you withdraw within the first three months after enrolling, you must repay everything you received. Withdrawing after the final announcement means you cannot apply for GKS again.

## Language

- Everyone does the one-year Korean language programme, even if the degree is taught in English.
- You must reach **TOPIK level 3** during that year to start the degree. Some departments need **level 4**.
- If you already have TOPIK level 5 or 6 when you apply, you skip the language year.
- TOPIK and English tests (TOEFL, IELTS) are **optional** for applying. They earn points, and a few departments require a score, so check the University Information file.

## How to apply (next round)

1. Read the new guidelines and the University Information file as soon as they are published.
2. Create an account on Study in Korea: <https://www.studyinkorea.go.kr/>
3. Complete the application form, personal statement and study plan online and upload your documents.
4. Choose your universities and departments carefully. After final selection you cannot change your field or your university.
5. If you pass the first round, send the **original, authenticated** documents by the date the embassy gives you.

**Documents:** application form, personal statement, study plan, **one recommendation letter** (dated within one year of the deadline, from someone who knows your studies, such as a teacher or head teacher), the applicant agreement, a medical assessment form and a consent form. You also need proof of your and your parents' citizenship and family relationship, your high school certificate and transcript, and your passport copy if the other documents do not show citizenship. Up to five certificates of awards or activities are optional.

The main certificates must be **apostilled or consular confirmed**. If a document is not in English or Korean it needs a certified translation. Ask the Korean embassy in Tanzania which authentication your Tanzanian documents need and how long it takes. Do not leave this to the last week.

## How you are selected

NIIED first checks eligibility (citizenship, education level, grades). Then the committee looks at your documents and holds an **oral interview**. It considers your academic results, language, activities, contribution to society, potential and how complete your documents are. Extra points go to science and engineering applicants and to anyone with TOPIK level 3 or above. Applicants from low-income or underprivileged families are given preference in the overall judgement.

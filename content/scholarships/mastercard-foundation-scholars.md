## What it is

The Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program gives full scholarships to young Africans who are academically strong but cannot afford university. It is run through **partner universities** in Africa and abroad. The Foundation calls it "more than just a scholarship": scholars also get mentoring, counselling and leadership training, and are expected to give back to their communities.

## The most important point

**You cannot apply to the Foundation directly.** The Foundation says: "you must apply directly through the Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program-affiliated institution you wish to attend." You can apply to as many partners as you wish. Being admitted to a university does not guarantee a scholarship, and the partner makes the decision.

## Is Tanzania included?

The programme is for African nationals, and there is no Tanzania-only rule against you. Two cautions:

- The Foundation's pages do not list eligible countries. Each partner decides, so **confirm that your partner accepts Tanzanian citizens** before you spend time on an application.
- Only some partners name Tanzania. For example, McGill University lists Tanzania among its eligible countries. UCT and Cambridge ask for citizens of an African country and do not list countries.

## Who can apply

According to the Foundation:

- You are an African national, academically strong and committed to helping others.
- You show leadership and a wish to give back to society.
- Financial need counts. Women, people with disabilities and forcibly displaced young people are especially encouraged.
- **Age:** 29 or under when you apply for a bachelor's degree, 35 or under for a master's degree.

Each partner adds its own rules. For example, McGill and Cambridge ask for a first degree, and Cambridge asks for a first-class degree for most courses.

## What it covers

The Foundation's FAQ lists tuition, accommodation, books and study materials, mentoring, counselling, leadership development and a return air ticket to your home country if necessary.

**The exact package depends on the partner.** The University of Pretoria, for example, lists meals, a monthly stipend, textbooks, medical aid and visa costs as well. Cambridge lists tuition and a monthly maintenance allowance. Always read your partner's page.

## How to apply

1. Open the Foundation's page and read the list of partners: <https://mastercardfdn.org/apply>
2. Pick partners that offer your course and accept Tanzanian citizens. Many Africa-based partners do not require SAT or TOEFL, but some partners do.
3. Go to **that partner's own Scholars Program page** and read the deadline, documents and steps.
4. Get your documents ready and ask for [recommendation letters](/guides/recommendation-letters-and-your-cv/) early. Late or incomplete applications are not considered.
5. Submit on the partner's official site. If you win a place abroad, read our [student visa basics](/guides/student-visa-basics/) and check that your [passport](/guides/getting-your-passport-in-tanzania/) is valid.

For most universities you must **apply for admission to the university and for the scholarship**. UCT, Pretoria and ALCHE all work this way.

> **Fees and scams:** some partners charge an application or admission fee. The Foundation says the fee is **reimbursed if you are selected**, and that it **never solicits funding or asks individuals for payment**. The Foundation also warns that some Facebook groups that advertise recruitment and ask for fees are not linked to it. Pay only on the university's official website, never to a person or a group. Report suspected fraud to privacy@mastercardfdn.org. See our guide on [how to spot scams](/guides/how-scholarships-work-and-how-to-spot-scams/).

## Prepare now

The programme looks for leadership and service, so your [personal statement](/guides/how-to-write-a-personal-statement/) should give real examples of how you helped others. Keep your certificates and results ready. Partners also ask for proof of financial need, such as documents about your family's income, so start collecting them early.

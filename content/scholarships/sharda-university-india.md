> **Read this first:** This is a **partly funded** option. It lowers your tuition bill. It does not pay for your living costs. Before you apply, work out the full yearly cost and how you will pay it. As of 19 November 2026 we could not find a published deadline or full fee table.

## What it is

Sharda University is a private university in Greater Noida, near Delhi, India. It teaches students from many countries. Its international pages say the campus has students from more than 95 nationalities.

Sharda gives **merit-based scholarships** to international students. In plain words: the better your final school results, the bigger the discount on your tuition fee. You do not fill in a separate scholarship form. You apply for admission, and in the application you upload your transcripts so the university can check which discount you qualify for.

This is not a government scholarship. Nobody pays your living costs.

## Who can apply

From Sharda's international pages (the Nigeria and Sierra Leone country pages):

- You must be an **international applicant** (not an Indian citizen applying through the Indian route).
- You need at least **50% in your qualifying examination** (your final secondary school exam, such as A-level or the equivalent in your country).
- You must have completed the **prerequisite subjects** for the programme you want. For example, an engineering programme will expect mathematics and science.

**Not confirmed:** Sharda does not publish a list of eligible countries, and we could not find a Tanzania-specific page. The pages we read say the scholarship rules apply to all international applicants. Sharda has also run country-specific schemes, for example one for Malawi (see Past schemes below).

## What it covers

- **Tuition fees only.** The Nigeria page says scholarships cover tuition fees.
- Sharda says every programme carries a **minimum assured scholarship of 20%**.
- The Nigeria page also mentions an **assured 50%** for PhD, M.Tech and some bachelor's programmes. It does not list which bachelor's programmes.
- The Sierra Leone page says "up to 50%" for degree programmes, except "council programmes" (programmes controlled by a national regulator).
- If you qualify for more than one benefit, you get **only the highest one**. They do not add together.

**Not covered:** accommodation, food, flights, visa, insurance, books and personal costs. The pages list several hostel types (AC, non-AC and apartments) but print no prices.

> **Careful:** "Up to 100%" appears in Sharda's marketing. The only official detail we found for 100% is an older scheme (see below). Do not assume you will get more than the minimum 20%. Ask for the exact percentage **in your offer letter** before you pay anything.

## Past schemes (for context)

- **Sharda International Scholarship Programme (SISP), 2022-23.** The official page then said 1,000 scholarships worth USD 9 million. It required 60% overall and 60% in English, and an online entrance test. The page lists two versions of the award table that do not match, so we do not repeat the numbers. We could not confirm SISP still runs.
- **Sharda Malawi scholarship, 2023.** An MoU with Malawi's Ministry of Education. News reports said up to 50% tuition waiver for 50 places, with a minimum GPA of 8.5 each year to keep it. Applications closed on 30 May 2023. This was for Malawians only.

## Key dates

- **Intake and deadline:** not stated on the pages we could read. The country pages say "Admissions Open 2026-27" with no closing date.
- **Indian-route page:** sharda.ac.in/scholarship shows "Admission Closed for Session 2026" for Indian applicants. This is a different route from the international one.
- **Visa:** Sharda says Indian student visas generally take **3 to 8 weeks**, so apply as soon as you get your admission or visa letter.

## Documents you need

The pages we read do not list every document. These are the ones they confirm:

- [ ] Your final secondary school transcripts (needed to check scholarship eligibility)
- [ ] Passport-size photo and signature (uploaded in the form)
- [ ] Passport (needed for the visa)
- [ ] Application fee payment
- [ ] Original transcripts and documents, shown for verification when you arrive on campus
- [ ] Other documents the form asks for at the time

## How to apply, step by step

The Sierra Leone page describes three steps (1 to 3 below). Steps 4 and 5 are what normally follows:

1. **Fill in the application form.** Choose your programme and specialisation. You get a System ID. Keep it safe.
2. **Complete your details.** Add your education details, upload your photo, signature and documents. Then do the scholarship check by attaching your transcripts. Every field must be filled.
3. **Pay the application fee.** That page lists **USD 500**. The Indian-route page lists INR 1,500, so confirm which one applies to you before paying.
4. **Wait for your offer or admission letter.** It should state your scholarship percentage.
5. **Apply for your student visa** at the Indian embassy or high commission, then arrive and have your original documents verified.

> **Careful:** Apply only on Sharda's own websites (sharda.ac.in and global.sharda.ac.in). Sharda's own page warns about third parties who claim to offer admission. Many "free scholarship help" sites are agents. Never pay an agent to "secure" a scholarship.

## How you are selected

Awards are **merit-based**. Sharda checks your transcripts and gives the discount that matches your marks. The exact marks bands are not published in the pages we could read.

To keep the scholarship, Sharda says you must show **regular attendance, academic progress and good conduct**. In the older SISP scheme, the discount covered the first year only, and later years depended on your results. Ask whether your award is for one year or all years.

## Preparing a strong application

- Aim for the highest marks you can. The percentage of your discount depends on them.
- Get your final transcripts ready early.
- Choose a programme where you meet the subject prerequisites.
- Ask Sharda to send the **full yearly cost** in writing: tuition after discount, hostel, food, insurance and other fees.
- Check that your home country will **recognise the degree**, especially for medicine, nursing, pharmacy, law and engineering. Ask your country's professional regulator before you apply. We did not research this for any country.

## Timeline: what to do and when

- **9 to 12 months before:** Decide the programme. Ask your regulator whether the degree will be recognised. Plan how you will pay living costs.
- **6 months before:** Open the application on Sharda's site. Upload transcripts and pay the fee.
- **After the offer:** Read the scholarship percentage and the fee table carefully before you accept.
- **As soon as you have the visa letter:** Apply for the visa (3 to 8 weeks).
- **Arrival:** Bring original documents for verification.

## Common mistakes

- Treating this as a full scholarship and then being unable to pay living costs.
- Paying an agent.
- Not asking for the full cost in writing.
- Not asking whether the discount continues every year.
- Not checking whether your home country recognises the degree.

## FAQ

### Is this a fully funded scholarship?
No. It is a tuition discount. The pages we read say it covers tuition only.

### What is the smallest discount?
Sharda says a minimum assured scholarship of 20% on all programmes.

### Can I get 100%?
Some older schemes offered up to 100% for a small number of students, and Sharda's marketing says "up to 100%". We could not confirm the current rules for 100%.

### Do I need to take an entrance exam?
The older SISP scheme used an online test. The current international pages we read do not mention one. Confirm with Sharda.

### Is there an English test requirement?
The Sierra Leone page does not state an English test or score. Check your programme's requirement before you apply.

### Is Tanzania included?
We could not find a Tanzania-specific page. Sharda's pages say the rules apply to all international applicants.

## What it is

Yale University is in New Haven, USA. It does not run a separate scholarship competition. Instead, **every admitted student gets a need-based financial aid offer**, and Yale pays it as a "Yale Scholarship": a grant that never has to be repaid.

Two rules matter most for you:

- **Need-blind admission.** Yale decides on admission without looking at your family's ability to pay, and says this applies whatever your citizenship. Asking for aid does not lower your chance.
- **Full need met, no loans.** Yale says its offers meet 100% of demonstrated need, regardless of citizenship or immigration status, and loans are not part of the first offer.

Yale is very selective. This is a real chance for a strong student, but it is not a sure award. Apply to other universities as well, such as the ones in our [scholarship list](/scholarships/).

## Is Tanzania included?

Yes, in the sense that Yale accepts applicants from every country. Yale's international page says all applicants are judged through the same process and fill in the same applications, whatever their nationality. There is no list of countries and no quota by country on the pages we read.

Yale's pages do not say how Tanzanian national exam results (Form Four and Form Six) are treated. **This is not confirmed.** Ask the admissions office before you plan: <https://admissions.yale.edu/contact>

## What it covers

Yale says the amount depends on your family's financial situation: income, assets, family size and special circumstances. So **the scholarship may cover everything or only part of the cost**. Read this as "fully funded for families who need it", not as a promise of a free place for every student.

What Yale says on its own pages:

- For families with the highest need (Yale describes a "zero parent share"), the scholarship covers the full cost of tuition and fees, housing, food and travel. Those students also get hospital insurance cover and a **2,000 US dollar start-up grant** in the first year. Yale's page names an income below 100,000 US dollars as the typical case, which is written with US families in mind.
- For international students, Yale says aid offers include allowances for housing, meals and travel to and from campus twice a year. Aid also takes into account the difference between the US economy and your home country's economy.
- Travel money is not paid separately. It is counted in the scholarship that is applied to your Yale bill.
- Hospital insurance support is offered to the highest-need students and international citizens who qualify.
- Yale does **not** give merit or sports scholarships. Every dollar of aid is based on need.
- International students do not have to reapply for aid every year.

You can get a personal cost estimate from Yale's calculator: <https://finaid.yale.edu/estimate>

## When to apply

As of **18 November 2026**, for study starting in 2027:

- **Single-Choice Early Action closed on 1 November 2026.** If you have not applied, you cannot use this round.
- **Regular Decision: 2 January.** The official page does not print the year. It is the round for the 2027 intake. Decisions come in late March and you reply by 1 May.
- **Financial aid priority dates (printed by Yale for 2027-28):** 15 February 2027 for Regular Decision. For international applicants who applied through Early Action, it was 1 December 2026.
- Yale says that if your aid file is not finished by the priority date, you are still eligible and your offer will not be reduced because it was late. Still, finish early so your aid decision arrives with your admission decision.

The 1 December date is only for students who already applied early. For most Tanzanian students reading this now, the date that matters is **2 January**.

## How to apply

1. Choose **one** application platform: the Common Application, the Coalition Application on SCOIR, or the QuestBridge Application. You send one application only.
2. On that application, **say that you want to apply for financial aid.** Yale then contacts you with the documents it needs.
3. Make sure these reach Yale: letters from **two teachers** (recent core subjects such as languages, maths, sciences or social studies) and **one counsellor**, and a **school report with an official transcript**. If your school has no counsellor, Yale says to ask a school administrator instead.
4. Send **SAT or ACT scores**. Yale requires them from all first-year applicants, including international ones. Scores from national exams do not replace them. SAT and ACT sittings in January or February are generally in time for Regular Decision.
5. If you are not a native English speaker and have **not** had at least two years of secondary school taught in English, also send an English test: TOEFL, IELTS, Cambridge English, Duolingo English Test or InitialView. Yale says its most competitive applicants usually score IELTS 7 or higher, Duolingo 120 or higher, and Cambridge 185 or higher. For TOEFL iBT, it names 5 or higher on the new scale (tests taken after 21 January 2026).
6. All documents must be in English. If a document is in another language, it needs a **certified translation**. Yale does not accept translations you make yourself.
7. Watch your **Yale Admissions Status Portal** after you apply. Your financial aid checklist appears there, and Yale can add new items, so check it often.

**Application fee:** Yale does not give its own fee waivers. You can ask for a waiver through the Common App or Coalition Application if you qualify. The QuestBridge application is always free.

## The financial aid application

- Yale says international applicants do not have to send the **CSS Profile** unless Yale asks for it. Do not pay for the CSS Profile before Yale tells you to.
- Yale does **not** accept the International Student Financial Aid Application (ISFAA) instead of its own forms.
- Yale tells international applicants who ask for aid what to send. Early Action applicants were contacted from mid-November. Your checklist in the portal is the one to follow.
- Parents' financial information is needed from both parents, even if they are divorced or separated. You can ask for an exception for a non-custodial parent in difficult cases. The exception is not guaranteed.
- If your checklist lists a document your family does not have, contact the aid office. Yale says checklist items can be changed or waived once documents are received.

Questions about aid can go to: undergrad.fa@yale.edu

## Beware of scams

Yale will never ask you to pay to be considered for a scholarship. Only use the web addresses listed here (yale.edu, commonapp.org, coalitionforcollegeaccess.org, collegeboard.org). Read [how scholarships work and how to spot scams](/guides/how-scholarships-work-and-how-to-spot-scams/).

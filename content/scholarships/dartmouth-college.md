## What it is

Dartmouth College is in Hanover, New Hampshire, USA. It does not run a separate scholarship competition. Instead, **every admitted student who shows need gets a financial aid offer**, and Dartmouth pays it as a "Dartmouth Scholarship": money that does not have to be repaid.

Two rules matter most for you:

- **Need-blind admission for international students.** Dartmouth's admissions office announced in 2022 that it extended need-blind admission to all international citizens, so financial need is not a factor in the decision. This announcement is the page we read, and it has not been updated since 2022.
- **Full need met, no required loan.** Dartmouth says it meets 100% of demonstrated need for every admitted undergraduate, whatever their citizenship, and that the package never includes a required student loan. Need is met with scholarship or grant money plus student employment.

Dartmouth is extremely selective. This is a real chance for a strong student, but it is not a sure award. Apply to other universities as well, such as the ones in our [scholarship list](/scholarships/).

## Is Tanzania included?

Yes, in the sense that Dartmouth accepts applicants from every country. The official pages we read have no list of countries and no quota by country. The financial aid office says your **parents' country of residence** decides which documents you must send. Tanzania falls under "Parents Living in Other Countries" (see "How to apply for aid" below).

Dartmouth's pages do not say how Tanzanian Form Four and Form Six results are treated. **This is not confirmed.** Ask the admissions office before you plan: apply@dartmouth.edu

## What it covers

Dartmouth says your need is the cost of education minus what your family can contribute. So **the scholarship may cover nearly everything or only part of the cost**. Read this as "fully funded for families who need it", not as a promise of a free place for every student.

What Dartmouth says on its own pages:

- **Families earning under 125,000 US dollars a year** (with typical assets) can expect a package with **no parent contribution**. These students pay their share from summer or leave-term earnings, a part-time campus job and/or personal savings.
- **Families earning 125,000 to 175,000 US dollars** can expect scholarships that cover the full cost of tuition.
- **There is no income cut-off** for scholarship consideration.
- Students who get aid are expected to contribute a minimum each year. The page says this comes from leave-term earnings of 1,000 to 3,000 US dollars a year plus a percentage of the student's own assets.
- Dartmouth Scholarships range from **1,000 to over 80,000 US dollars**.
- Many aid students are linked to one of Dartmouth's endowed scholarship funds. This is not extra money, and no separate application is needed.
- Scholarships from outside organisations must be reported to the financial aid office. Above a set amount they reduce your Dartmouth Scholarship dollar for dollar.

**Cost of attendance for 2026-27 (three terms), as printed by Dartmouth:**

- Tuition 71,697 US dollars
- Fees 2,157
- Housing 13,032
- Food 8,496
- Books and supplies 1,005
- Personal expenses 2,040
- Total budget **98,427 US dollars**

On top of that: student **health insurance 5,216 US dollars** (unless you prove you have comparable cover), a **computer** (basic package 1,700 US dollars), and a one-time fee of 269 US dollars for records and class dues plus 250 for meals during orientation.

**Travel:** Dartmouth says each student is responsible for their own travel and that these costs are usually paid by the student or family. The aid budget includes a travel allowance (at least 250 US dollars, based on distance), but this is a budgeting figure, not a flight ticket. **Plan how you will pay for your flights from Tanzania.** Some websites say Dartmouth pays for flights. Dartmouth's own cost page does not say this.

You can get a personal estimate from Dartmouth's net price calculators: <https://financialaid.dartmouth.edu/cost-attendance/net-price-calculator>

## When to apply

As of **18 November 2026**, for study starting in 2027:

- **Binding Early Decision closed on 1 November 2026.** If you have not applied, you cannot use this round.
- **Regular Decision: 1 January.** The official pages do not print the year. It is the round for the 2027 intake. The page says 11:59 p.m. in your local time. The application fee or fee waiver is also due on 1 January. Decisions come in late March or early April and you reply by 1 May.
- **Financial aid documents for Regular Decision: 1 February.** The aid office lists the CSS Profile, tax documents and other documents as due on this date.
- One official page (for QuestBridge applicants) gives 2 January for Regular Decision. The main timeline says 1 January. **Use 1 January**, and finish earlier if you can.

Everything below needs time, especially the SAT or ACT, English test and your family's income documents. **Start now.**

## How to apply

1. Create a **Common Application** account and add Dartmouth to "My Colleges". All first-year applicants use the Common App: <https://www.commonapp.org/>
2. Complete the **Dartmouth Writing Supplement** (required).
3. Make sure these reach Dartmouth: the **secondary school report with transcript and school profile**, a **counsellor evaluation**, and **two teacher evaluations**. Dartmouth has an FAQ for students whose school has no counsellor: <https://admissions.dartmouth.edu/glossary-question/if-my-school-doesnt-have-counselor-who-should-i-ask-write-recommendation>
4. Send your **standardized testing** (see below).
5. If English is not your first language and your schooling has **not** been in English for at least two years, also send an **English test** (see below).
6. A **peer recommendation** is recommended but not required. An alumni interview is optional and only happens if an alum contacts you. Interviews are not required.
7. Pay the application fee through the Common App, or **ask for a fee waiver** there if the fee would cause unusual hardship. Dartmouth says asking for a waiver does not hurt your application.

## Testing and English

**Standardized testing is required.** Because you attend school outside the USA, Dartmouth lets you meet the rule in one of five ways:

1. SAT or ACT scores (scores from several sittings are combined)
2. Three AP exam results
3. Predicted or final IB Diploma results
4. Predicted or final British A-Level results
5. Final results from a standardized national exam, such as WASSCE or KCSE. The page does not name Tanzania's exams, so **we cannot confirm that ACSEE counts**. Ask the admissions office. The page also says predicted national exam scores do not count, and if your national results will only arrive after February you must choose a different option.

For most Tanzanian applicants this means **the SAT or ACT is the safest route**. Check the College Board and ACT websites for a test centre near you and book early. Dartmouth does not set a minimum score.

**English test:** Dartmouth accepts TOEFL, IELTS, Duolingo or Cambridge English. It has no minimum score, but it says most successful applicants score above 7 on IELTS, above 135 on Duolingo or above 185 on Cambridge English. For TOEFL taken on or after 21 January 2026, most successful applicants score 5 or above. TOEFL Essentials and the MyBest feature are not accepted.

## How to apply for aid

You apply for aid **at the same time** as for admission. Asking for aid does not change the admission decision, according to Dartmouth's need-blind policy.

For parents living outside the USA and Canada, the aid office asks for:

1. The **CSS Profile**, online at <https://cssprofile.collegeboard.org/>. Dartmouth's CSS code is **3351**.
2. **Your parents' income and tax documents**, sent directly to the financial aid office.

Details to know:

- Dartmouth says it **does not give CSS Profile fee waivers**. If the CSS fee is a serious hardship, you can instead download and complete the **International Student Financial Aid Application (ISFAA)** and email it to **financial.aid@dartmouth.edu**. Dartmouth says the ISFAA is the equivalent of the CSS Profile and does not change your eligibility. You still send the same income documents.
- The aid office page does not say whether documents must be translated into English. **Not confirmed.** Ask the aid office before you pay for translations.
- If your family has no tax return, ask the aid office what to send. Do not guess.
- Keep the 1 February date for aid documents in mind, and send them as early as you can.

Questions about aid can go to: financial.aid@dartmouth.edu

## Beware of scams

Dartmouth will never ask you to pay to be considered for a scholarship. Only use the web addresses listed here (dartmouth.edu, commonapp.org, collegeboard.org). Read [how scholarships work and how to spot scams](/guides/how-scholarships-work-and-how-to-spot-scams/).

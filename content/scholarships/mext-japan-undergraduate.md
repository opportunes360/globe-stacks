## What it is

The MEXT scholarship is paid for by Japan's Ministry of Education (MEXT). The undergraduate track gives you a place at a Japanese university for a full bachelor's degree. Tanzanians apply through the **embassy recommendation** route: the Embassy of Japan in Dar es Salaam does the first screening, and MEXT in Tokyo makes the final decision.

You cannot choose your university. MEXT places you.

## Fields of study

You choose up to three choices from **one** group. You cannot change your field after you submit.

- **Social Sciences and Humanities A:** Law, Politics, Pedagogy, Sociology, Literature, History, Japanese Language and similar subjects.
- **Social Sciences and Humanities B:** Economics, Business Administration and related subjects such as Accounting.
- **Natural Sciences A:** Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry and most engineering fields (electrical, mechanical, civil, information, architecture and others), plus Biotechnology.
- **Natural Sciences B:** Agriculture, Veterinary Medicine, Forestry, Food Science, Fisheries, Pharmacy, Nursing, Biology and related fields.
- **Natural Sciences C:** Medicine and Dentistry.

Art fields, including music, are not offered. You cannot pick both Natural Sciences A and B, because their exam subjects differ.

## What it covers

- **Tuition, entrance exam fee and matriculation fee:** waived.
- **Monthly allowance:** 117,000 yen. In some designated regions of Japan there is an extra 2,000 or 3,000 yen. The amount can change from year to year.
- **Flights:** an economy ticket to Japan, and a ticket home if you graduate and return by the end of the scholarship. You pay your own domestic travel, airport taxes, insurance and baggage.
- **Accommodation:** you pay for it from your allowance.
- **Health insurance:** you must join Japan's National Health Insurance when you arrive.
- **First payment:** comes 1 to 1.5 months after you arrive. The guidelines suggest bringing about US$2,000 to cover that gap.

The guidelines do not give family allowances. If you have dependants, the guidelines advise arriving alone first and finding housing before they join.

## How long it lasts

Most students first do **one year of Japanese language study** (in the 2027 guidelines, at ABK College for the social sciences and humanities, and at Osaka University for the natural sciences). After that you start your degree. All university classes are taught in Japanese.

- Total scholarship for most degrees: **five years** (April 2027 to March 2032 in the 2027 round).
- Medicine, Dentistry, Veterinary Medicine and six-year Pharmacy: **seven years**.

If you cannot finish the language year or cannot enter a university afterwards, the scholarship is cancelled and you must return home. You cannot transfer between universities.

## Who could apply last time

- Citizens of Tanzania. Foreign nationals cannot apply through the Tanzania embassy.
- Born on or after **2 April 2002**. Exceptions are very limited.
- 12 years of school education completed by March 2027, or an equivalent qualification. Current Form Six students were allowed if they finished and could show proof by March 2027.
- Willing to learn Japanese and study in Japanese.
- A doctor must complete the embassy's medical form.
- You cannot apply if you are in the military, have received a MEXT scholarship before, or have applied to another MEXT programme in the same year.

You do **not** need a Japanese or English language certificate. If you have one, it must be less than two years old.

## When to apply

The Embassy of Japan in Tanzania ran the 2027 round like this:

- **Applications:** 21 April to 21 May 2026.
- **Written exam:** 3 June 2026. **Interview:** 23 June 2026 (both "subject to change"). Exams and interviews are in person, not online.
- **Final results:** announced by the embassy after February 2027. Successful students were expected to travel to Japan in April 2027.

The embassy page now says applications are closed. The 2026 round ran from 15 April to 30 May 2025, so the pattern is mid-April to May. **This is a pattern, not a date.** Check the embassy page and its news from March 2027.

## How to apply (last round's process)

1. Read the official undergraduate guidelines and the embassy's FAQ and document checklist on the embassy page.
2. Download and complete the forms. You can type or handwrite them.
3. Collect your documents (see below). Write the document number in the top right corner of each first page.
4. Put everything in **one envelope** with the check sheet on top. **Do not staple.** The embassy said extra copies were not required.
5. Post it or hand-deliver it to the Embassy of Japan in Dar es Salaam. **Email and online applications are not accepted.**
6. If you pass document screening, you are called for the written exam and interview. If you hear nothing from the embassy by the end of May, treat your application as unsuccessful. The embassy does not tell you why.
7. After you pass the written exam, submit the medical certificate.

Documents are **not returned**, so do not send originals if a certified copy is allowed.

**Documents needed:**

- Application form (that year's version), with a photo
- Academic transcripts for all years of school
- Graduation certificate, or a certificate that you are about to graduate
- Recommendation letter from a class teacher or the principal of your last school (any format)
- Medical certificate on the official form (after the written exam)
- Certificate of enrolment, only if you are at a university
- Language certificate, only if you have one

**Exam subjects** depend on your field. Social sciences and humanities: Japanese, English and Mathematics. Natural Sciences A: Japanese, English, Mathematics, Chemistry and Physics. Natural Sciences B and C: Japanese, English, Mathematics, Chemistry and Biology. Calculators are not allowed. The embassy does not answer questions about the exam content, so read the guidelines carefully.

**Important:** passing the embassy screening does not guarantee a scholarship. MEXT chooses the final students, and objections to your university placement are not accepted. The embassy does not publish how many students are selected.

## Conditions to keep it

You must stay enrolled and in good standing, follow the pledge you sign, and take part in school and community activities. The scholarship can be stopped, and in some cases you may have to repay money, for false statements, poor grades, suspension or expulsion, breaking visa rules or taking another conflicting scholarship.

## Contact

The embassy answers by email only: embassyofjapan_TZ@dr.mofa.go.jp. It does not give individual advice or answer questions already covered in its FAQ. Applications go to the embassy by post or hand delivery, so be careful with anyone who asks for money to "process" one. See [how scholarships work and how to spot scams](/guides/how-scholarships-work-and-how-to-spot-scams/).

## Prepare now

Application packs are long and strict. Start reading the 2027 guidelines now, begin learning Japanese, practise mathematics, chemistry and English for the written exam, and prepare your [personal statement](/guides/how-to-write-a-personal-statement/) and [recommendation letters](/guides/recommendation-letters-and-your-cv/). Make sure your school transcripts and certificates are easy to get, and check your [passport](/guides/getting-your-passport-in-tanzania/) is valid.
